# BGM integration v19

- Uploaded JPC tracks 1–4 play during launched balls 1–20, 21–40, 41–60, and 61–70. A 24/25-pocket reach overrides the numbered track; it persists across ball ranges. At 25/25 or JPC completion music stops. Restart begins JPC1. Normal return restores BET music.
- All 20 uploaded vocal Wonder tracks (generations 1–9, three from each of 1/2 and two from each of 3–9) form the normal game pool. Choose once while preparing each game, excluding the immediately preceding track; retain that choice through the game's end. BET music remains separate.
- Original uploaded MP3 files are copied byte-for-byte. Old Wonder assets are retained, but excluded from the new pool.
- `assets/bgm-player.js` supplies the music transport. For the 25 new files it decodes only requested tracks, caches at most two completed buffers, and loops with AudioBufferSourceNode.loopStart/loopEnd on the sample clock. Pause/resume retains position; source/request generations discard old asynchronous loads. BET continues using the existing media element and common gain/fade controls. Number announcements use their separate bus.
- `assets/audio/bgm-loops.json` records source title, loop bounds, method and analysis score. Eleven tracks have waveform/spectral repeat matches. Fourteen lack a sufficiently reliable repeat match: these retain the whole audible song and use a 120ms boundary blend, rather than inventing an internal musical cut. Matched loops use 25–60ms pre-roll blending. All loops preserve the intro on the first pass. The loop's pre-roll blend preserves the repeat period and sample continuity; original MP3 files are not edited.
- Validation: actual Chromium audio decoding for all 25 files, loop bounds and exact seam pre-roll samples, bounded cache, normal game's stable random selection, crossing a real loop boundary without replacing the source node, pause/resume, and reset. JPC boundary balls 1/20/21/40/41/60/61/70, reach priority, mute, jackpot/completion stop, restart and normal return. These are automated audio/transport checks, not an iPhone speaker listening test; fallback musical phrasing may benefit from user-specified cue times.
